# The app stays free and open; the paid product is a closed-source Installer

Nag Killer (the app and its Watchdog) stays MIT-licensed, and the Manual Install stays documented in the README. Money comes from the Installer, sold with the Signed Build and its updates. The Installer's own source is closed and kept out of this public repo; only its ready-built downloads are sold, from an unlisted link given after payment. We chose this over closing the app or giving the Installer away: the app is already public under MIT, so closing it protects nothing, while a public Installer would leave nothing to pay for. Support is best effort by email, because this is low-maintenance pocket money, not a staffed business.

## Consequences

- Anyone may still build and sell their own installer from the MIT app code. The Installer competes on convenience, trust and updates, not exclusivity.
- The Installer ships the MIT app, so the download must include the app's MIT licence notice.
- Selling starts with the Supported Car only (Australian Sealion 7). Other models wait until each one is proven.
